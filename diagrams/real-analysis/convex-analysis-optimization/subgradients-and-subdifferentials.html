1 · Formal definitions

A global affine lower support

A subgradient is not merely a local tangent approximation. Its affine function must lie below the convex function on the entire domain.

Subgradient

The right-hand side is a global affine minorant touching \(f\) at \(x_0\).

Subdifferential

For convex \(f\), this is a closed convex set of slopes or vectors.

Convex Fermat rule

Zero slope is both necessary and sufficient for global optimality.

6 · Core theorems

Subdifferentials as first-order geometry

The derivative rules of smooth convex analysis survive in set-valued form.

Existence on the relative interior

A supporting hyperplane to the epigraph with last component \(-1\) produces a subgradient.

Differentiable case

The usual tangent plane is the unique affine support.

Monotonicity

The subdifferential mapping of a convex function is monotone.

Sum rule

Under the standard qualification condition, subgradients add by Minkowski sum.

10 · What can go wrong?

Convexity makes the inequality global

Without convexity, tangent slopes and stationary points lose their global meaning.

A tangent line need not be a subgradient

For a nonconvex function, the derivative line may cross above the graph elsewhere.

Correction. The subgradient inequality is global, not merely first-order local contact.

Zero derivative need not imply a minimum without convexity

For \(f(x)=x^3\), \(f'(0)=0\), but \(0\) is not a minimizer.

Missing hypothesis. The convex Fermat rule depends on convexity.

The subdifferential may be empty at a domain boundary

A proper convex function always has subgradients on the relative interior of its domain, but boundary points may require extra closedness or regularity.

Correct existence region. Use \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}f)\).

The exact sum rule needs qualification

Without a relative-interior intersection condition, only one inclusion may be immediate.

Regularity condition. A standard assumption is \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}f)\cap\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}g)\neq\varnothing\).
11 · Common mistakes

Precision traps

The most common errors replace a set-valued object by an ordinary derivative too early.

Incorrect: “The subgradient is the slope of the tangent line.” Correct only at differentiable points; at a corner there may be an interval of valid supporting slopes.
Incorrect: “Any line through \((x_0,f(x_0))\) lying locally below the graph is a subgradient.” Correct: it must lie below the graph globally.
Incorrect: “\(\partial f(x)\) denotes a partial derivative.” Here it denotes the convex subdifferential, a set of vectors.
Incorrect: “\(0\in\partial f(x)\) gives only a local minimum.” For convex \(f\), it gives a global minimum.
Incorrect: “A singleton subdifferential always implies differentiability with no assumptions.” For proper convex functions on the relative interior of the domain, this is the relevant differentiability criterion.
