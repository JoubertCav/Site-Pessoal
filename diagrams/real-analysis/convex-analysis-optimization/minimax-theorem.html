Minimax theorem

One player minimizes after anticipating the maximizer; the other maximizes after anticipating the minimizer. The minimax theorem identifies hypotheses under which these two sequential values coincide and a saddle value emerges.

11 · What can go wrong?

Each hypothesis controls a distinct failure

Convexity, concavity, compactness, and semicontinuity are not interchangeable.

The squared-distance example has gap one

For \((x-y)^2\) on \([-1,1]^2\), the maximizer’s sections are convex, not concave.

Result. Max–min \(=0\), while min–max \(=1\).

Pure strategies may fail to have a value

Matching pennies has pure max–min \(-1\) and pure min–max \(1\).

Repair. Passing to mixed strategies convexifies the sets and yields value \(0\).

Values may fail to be attained

On open or unbounded strategy sets, the equality of extended values may coexist with no saddle point.

Role of compactness. It supplies convergent maximizing or minimizing subsequences.

Jumps can destroy closure of best-response values

Without lower semicontinuity in the minimizing variable or upper semicontinuity in the maximizing variable, limiting strategies may lose optimality.

Role of semicontinuity. It preserves the required inequality under limits.
12 · Common mistakes

Precision traps

Most errors come from reversing the universal inequality or confusing equality of values with existence of a saddle point.

Incorrect: “\(\inf\sup\le\sup\inf\) always.” Correct: \(\sup\inf\le\inf\sup\).
Incorrect: “Convexity in both variables is enough.” Correct: the minimizing variable should be convex; the maximizing variable should be concave.
Incorrect: “Equality of values automatically implies a saddle point.” Correct only when the extrema are attained by compatible strategies.
Incorrect: “Pure and mixed minimax values are the same.” Mixed strategies may close a pure-strategy gap.
Incorrect: “Sion’s theorem always requires both sets compact.” A standard version requires one set compact, with the appropriate convexity and semicontinuity assumptions.
