5 · Why affine dependence appears

Lift the points by one coordinate

Affine dependence in \(\mathbb R^n\) is ordinary linear dependence of the lifted vectors \((a_i,1)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}\).

With more than \(n+1\) lifted vectors in \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\), linear dependence is unavoidable.

Geometry

Invisible motion

The vector \(\alpha\) changes barycentric coordinates but not the represented point.

Feasibility

Preserve nonnegativity

Move only until the first positive-direction coefficient reaches zero.

Termination

Finite descent

Each step reduces the number of active coefficients by at least one, so the process ends.

Proof strategy

Dimension + feasible descent

Finite dimension creates a nontrivial null direction. Convexity turns that direction into a path inside the coefficient simplex. The first boundary hit removes one generator.

Reusable pattern. Find a representation with redundant variables, move within the fiber of the representation map, and hit a lower-dimensional face of the feasible set.
7 · Sharpness

Why \(n+1\) cannot generally be improved

An interior point of an \(n\)-simplex requires all \(n+1\) vertices.

If \(\bar v\) were a convex combination of only \(n\) vertices, it would lie in a proper face of the simplex, contradicting interiority.

Dimension 1

Intervals need two endpoints

An interior point of \([a,b]\) cannot be represented by one endpoint alone.

Dimension 2

Triangles need three vertices

A point in the interior of a triangle lies on no edge, so two vertices cannot suffice.

Dimension 3

Tetrahedra need four vertices

An interior point lies in no triangular face.

10 · What can go wrong?

Missing hypotheses and false strengthenings

Each counterexample isolates one essential feature of the theorem.

The \(n+1\) bound is sharp

The barycenter of an \(n\)-simplex needs every vertex.

Failed conclusion. No universal reduction to \(n\) points is possible.

No dimension-free finite bound

In an infinite-dimensional space, the averages \(x_m=\frac1m(e_1+\cdots+e_m)\) require all \(m\) affinely independent basis vectors in that representation.

Missing hypothesis. Finite ambient or affine dimension is essential for a uniform support bound.

Preserving the point is not enough

If \(\sum_i\alpha_i a_i=0\) but \(\sum_i\alpha_i\neq0\), then the modified coefficients no longer sum to one.

Proof break. The path leaves the coefficient simplex.

Affine combinations are not convex combinations

Moving too far along the null direction produces negative coefficients.

Correct stopping rule. Stop exactly at the first boundary time \(t_*\).
11 · Common mistakes

Proof-writing traps

Most errors arise from confusing linear and affine dependence or skipping feasibility.

Incorrect: “More than \(n\) points in \(\mathbb R^n\) are affinely dependent.” Correct: more than \(n+1\) points are necessarily affinely dependent.
Incorrect: “Linear dependence of \(a_i\) is exactly what is needed.” Correct: the coefficients must also sum to zero; equivalently, use the lifted vectors \((a_i,1)\).
Incorrect: “Choose any nonzero \(t\).” Correct: choose the first boundary time that preserves all coefficient inequalities.
Incorrect: “The theorem says the whole convex hull is one simplex.” Correct: each point may require a different simplex.
Incorrect: “The ambient dimension is always the sharp bound.” Correct: replace \(n\) by the affine dimension of the generating set whenever it is smaller.
