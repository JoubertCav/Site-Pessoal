11 · Examples

Canonical, structural, and boundary cases

The same definitions appear in analysis, optimization, probability, and functional analysis.

Canonical

Intervals and balls

Every interval in and every norm ball in a normed space is convex. The triangle inequality keeps convex combinations inside the ball.

Probability

The simplex

Probability vectors form a convex set. Mixing two distributions produces another distribution.

Function space

Convex functions as a convex cone

If and are convex functions, then every nonnegative linear combination is convex.

Optimization

Feasible regions

Intersections of affine half-spaces are convex. Linear constraints therefore generate convex polyhedra.

Boundary case

An open segment

The set is convex but has no extreme points. Extreme points need not exist without compactness or closedness.

Infinite dimension

Unit ball of a normed space

The closed unit ball is convex. In infinite-dimensional spaces it need not be compact, so finite-dimensional geometric intuition must be used carefully.

12 · What can go wrong?

Near-convex statements that are false

Each failure isolates a missing sign, quantifier, or structural hypothesis.

The union of convex sets need not be convex

Take two disjoint disks. Each disk contains its own segments, but a segment joining one disk to the other passes through points outside the union.

Proof break. Given and , neither convexity hypothesis applies to the pair because they need not belong to the same set.

A boundary point need not be extreme

On an edge of a square, every nonvertex boundary point is a nontrivial mixture of the two endpoints of that edge.

Correct distinction. Extreme points are geometrically irreducible. Boundary points merely fail to be interior.

Sum one is not enough for convexity

The coefficients and add to one, but usually lies beyond , outside the segment.

Missing hypothesis. Convex combinations require nonnegative coefficients. Without that sign condition, one obtains the affine hull.

Checking only one chosen weight is insufficient

A set may contain midpoints of some selected pairs while missing other points on the corresponding segments.

Quantifier failure. Convexity requires every pair and every , not a finite sample of tests.
13 · Common mistakes

Precision traps in convex geometry

The correction is often a single quantifier or coefficient restriction.

Incorrect: “Every point on the boundary of a convex set is extreme.” Correct: only points that admit no nontrivial two-point decomposition are extreme.
Incorrect: “The convex hull consists only of pairwise segments.” Correct: in dimension two, interiors of triangles are also required; generally finite convex combinations are needed.
Incorrect: “Affine and convex combinations are the same because both have coefficient sum one.” Correct: convex coefficients must also be nonnegative.
Incorrect: “The convex hull of an open set is always closed.” Correct: , which is not closed.
Incorrect: “Every convex set is the convex hull of its extreme points.” Correct: this requires additional hypotheses; compact convex sets in finite-dimensional spaces satisfy such a representation, while open convex sets may have no extreme points.
