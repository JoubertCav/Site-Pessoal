Norms and normed spaces

A norm turns a vector space into a geometry of size, distance, convergence, and boundedness. In finite dimensions, every norm generates the same topology. In infinite dimensions, this stability can fail dramatically.

Common mistakes

Statements that look plausible but are false

The correction often consists of adding exactly one missing hypothesis.

Incorrect: Every two norms are equivalent.
Correct: every two norms on the same finite-dimensional vector space are equivalent.
Incorrect: Equivalent norms must assign approximately the same numerical value to each vector.
Correct: only global two-sided multiplicative bounds are required.
Incorrect: A bounded closed set is compact in every normed space.
Correct: this is true in finite-dimensional normed spaces, not in general.
Incorrect: If a functional satisfies the triangle inequality and homogeneity, it is automatically a norm.
Correct: definiteness must still be verified; otherwise one may have only a seminorm.
Incorrect: The unit sphere is always compact.
Correct: compactness of the unit sphere characterizes finite-dimensionality among normed spaces.
