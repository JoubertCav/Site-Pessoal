1. The mathematical question

When is a linear map analytically stable?

Linearity alone does not prevent arbitrarily large amplification. Continuity is the requirement that small perturbations of the input remain small after applying the operator.

Algebra

Preserve combinations

The operator respects addition and scalar multiplication.

Geometry

Control amplification

One global constant controls every direction and every scale.

Analysis

Preserve limits

For linear maps, this is equivalent to boundedness.

11. Common mistakes

Tempting statements that are false

Each correction separates algebraic structure from topological structure.

Incorrect: every linear operator is continuous.

Correct only when the domain is finite-dimensional. In infinite dimensions, the derivative on polynomials under the sup norm is a standard counterexample.

Incorrect: bounded means the set is bounded.

Correct: bounded means bounded on the unit ball, equivalently . A nonzero linear operator has an unbounded full range whenever scalar multiplication is unrestricted.

Incorrect: the operator norm is always a maximum.

Correct: it is a supremum. Attainment follows in finite dimensions from compactness of the unit sphere, but may fail in infinite dimensions.

Incorrect: a closed kernel implies the operator is continuous.

Correct: continuity implies a closed kernel. The converse fails in general: the unbounded derivative on the polynomial space has the closed kernel of constant polynomials.

Incorrect: a bounded operator has closed range.

Correct: the range can be dense and nonclosed, as for .
