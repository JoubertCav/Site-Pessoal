1 · Formal language

Weak, strict, and supporting separation

The same functional \(x\mapsto\langle u,x\rangle\) produces different conclusions depending on whether the support intervals overlap, touch, or have a positive gap.

Weak separation

The two support intervals may touch. The separating hyperplane may meet one or both sets.

Strict separation

There is a positive scalar gap. Any level between the two support values defines a separating hyperplane.

Supporting hyperplane

The hyperplane touches the set without cutting through its interior.

7 · Why strict separation can fail

Disjoint closed convex sets may have zero distance

Closedness and disjointness alone do not force a positive margin when the sets are unbounded.

Counterexample

Both sets are nonempty, closed, convex, and disjoint. Yet \(\operatorname{dist}(A,B)=0\) because \(e^x\to0\) as \(x\to-\infty\).

What survives

9 · What can go wrong?

Each hypothesis performs a specific job

Remove convexity, closedness, or positive distance and a different part of the proof fails.

A single hyperplane may not separate a nonconvex set

Place two points on opposite sides of \(p\). Their nonconvex union excludes \(p\), but every hyperplane placing both points in one closed half-space also contains their segment, hence cannot strictly isolate \(p\).

Proof break. The segment \(q+t(x-q)\) may leave the set, so the variational inequality cannot be derived.

The nearest point may not exist

For the open unit ball and a boundary point \(p\), the distance is zero but unattained.

Proof break. There is no projection point \(q\in C\) from which to construct the normal.

Disjoint does not imply positive margin

The exponential epigraph and the \(x\)-axis are disjoint, closed, and convex but approach each other asymptotically.

Correct conclusion. Weak separation may remain, but strict support inequality fails.

The normal must be nonzero

With \(u=0\), every point has the same functional value, so no geometric distinction is certified.

Degenerate certificate. The equation \(\langle0,x\rangle=\alpha\) is either empty or the whole space.
10 · Common mistakes

Precision traps

Separation statements are sensitive to strict inequalities, attainment, and normalization.

Incorrect: “Disjoint closed convex sets are always strictly separated.” Correct: positive distance is sufficient; unbounded closed convex sets may be disjoint with distance zero.
Incorrect: “Weak separation means the hyperplane misses both sets.” Correct: it may touch one or both sets.
Incorrect: “The scalar \(\alpha\) is the certificate by itself.” Correct: the certificate is the pair \((u,\alpha)\), with \(u\neq0\).
Incorrect: “Any normal direction works.” Correct: the support intervals in that direction must be ordered.
Incorrect: “Strict pointwise inequalities automatically give a uniform margin.” Correct: for noncompact sets, a pointwise gap may have infimum zero; the support-gap formulation makes the uniform margin explicit.
