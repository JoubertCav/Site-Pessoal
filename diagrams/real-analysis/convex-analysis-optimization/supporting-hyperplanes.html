10 · What can go wrong?

Support does not imply uniqueness or smoothness

The contact set and the normal cone may be much larger than a single point or ray.

A supporting hyperplane may touch an edge

For a square and an axis-aligned normal, every point of one edge maximizes the objective.

Correction. Support identifies an exposed face, not necessarily an exposed point.

Interior points admit no nonzero support normal

A nonzero hyperplane through an interior point cuts a small ball around that point into two sides.

Consequence. \(N_C(x)=\{0\}\) for interior points of a full-dimensional convex set.

A corner has many supporting hyperplanes

At a polygon vertex, every normal inside the cone spanned by adjacent edge normals is valid.

Correction. Smoothness is what collapses the normal cone to a single ray.

Extreme need not imply exposed

Some extreme points of closed convex sets cannot be isolated as the unique maximizer of any linear functional.

Correct implication. Exposed points are extreme, but the converse fails in general.
11 · Common mistakes

Precision traps

The direction of inequalities and the distinction between faces and points are the main sources of error.

Incorrect: “A supporting hyperplane must intersect the set at exactly one point.” Correct: its contact set is an exposed face and may have positive dimension.
Incorrect: “Every boundary point has a unique normal.” Correct: uniqueness is a smoothness property, not merely a boundary property.
Incorrect: “The normal cone contains only unit vectors.” Correct: it is a cone, hence closed under multiplication by nonnegative scalars and always contains zero.
Incorrect: “\(u\in N_C(x)\) means \(x\) minimizes \(\langle u,\cdot\rangle\).” With the convention used here, it means \(x\) maximizes the functional.
Incorrect: “Any face is exposed.” Correct for polyhedra, but false for arbitrary closed convex sets.
