Formal objects

From a vector space to its space of measurements

The dual space contains only continuous linear functionals. In finite dimensions every linear functional is continuous; in infinite dimensions the distinction is essential.

Definition — continuous dual

Let \((X,\|\cdot\|)\) be a normed space over \(\mathbb F\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}\). Its continuous dual is

\[X^*=\mathcal L(X,\mathbb F)=\{\varphi:X\to\mathbb F:\varphi\text{ is linear and continuous}\}.\]

The dual norm is the operator norm of the scalar-valued map:

\[\|\varphi\|_* = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1}|\varphi(x)| = \sup_{x\ne 0}\frac{|\varphi(x)|}{\|x\|}.\]

Polar geometry

Why the dual norm has the conjugate exponent

The dual unit ball consists of all coefficient vectors whose supporting hyperplanes do not cut through the primal unit ball.

Primal \(\ell^1\)
\[\|x\|_1=|x_1|+|x_2|,\qquad \|\varphi_a\|_*=\|a\|_\infty.\]

A diamond has a square polar. The largest coordinate of \(a\) decides the maximal measurement.

Primal \(\ell^2\)
\[\|x\|_2=(|x_1|^2+|x_2|^2)^{1/2},\qquad \|\varphi_a\|_*=\|a\|_2.\]

The Euclidean ball is self-dual. Cauchy–Schwarz identifies the norming vector.

Primal \(\ell^\infty\)
\[\|x\|_\infty=\max_i|x_i|,\qquad \|\varphi_a\|_*=\|a\|_1.\]

A square has a diamond polar. Every sign can be chosen independently to maximize \(a\cdot x\).

Proof architecture

The theorems that organize the dual

Each proof is decomposed into the exact estimate, hypothesis, and geometric idea that moves the argument forward.

Theorem — continuity and boundedness
\[\varphi:X\to\mathbb F\text{ linear}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \varphi\text{ continuous}\iff \exists C\ge0:\ |\varphi(x)|\le C\|x\|.\]
\[|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|=|\varphi(x-y)|\le C\|x-y\|.\]

Reason: linearity converts increments into one vector; the bound makes \(\varphi\) Lipschitz.

\[\exists\delta>0:\ \|x\|<\delta\Longrightarrow |\varphi(x)|<1.\]

Hypothesis used: continuity at \(0\). For a linear map, continuity at one point is enough.

\[u=\frac{\delta}{2\|x\|}x,\qquad \|u\|=\frac\delta2<\delta.\]

Technique: homogeneity. The local estimate at zero is transported to every scale.

\[\frac{\delta}{2\|x\|}|\varphi(x)|=|\varphi(u)|<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\varphi(x)|<\frac{2}{\delta}\|x\|.\]

Conclusion: the functional is bounded with \(C=2/\delta\).

Canonical examples

Functionals across familiar spaces

The same abstract definition appears as a row vector, a point measurement, an integral, or a weighted series.

Matrix analogy

Row vectors on \(\mathbb R^n\)

\[\varphi_a(x)=a^\top x.\]
Every linear functional has this form.
The kernel is a hyperplane normal to \(a\).
Under \(\ell^p\), the dual norm is \(\|a\|_q\).
Evaluation

Point evaluation on \(C(K)\)

\[\delta_t(f)=f(t),\qquad \|\delta_t\|=1.\]
Bound: \(|f(t)|\le\|f\|_\infty\).
Equality is attained by the constant function \(1\).
The kernel consists of functions vanishing at \(t\).
Integration

Integral functional

\[I(f)=\int_a^b f(t)\,dt,\qquad \|I\|=b-a.\]
Bound: \(|I(f)|\le(b-a)\|f\|_\infty\).
The constant sign function attains equality.
Its kernel contains all continuous functions of zero mean.
Sequence spaces

Pairing \(\ell^p\) with \(\ell^q\)

\[\varphi_a(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nx_n.\]
Hölder gives \(|\varphi_a(x)|\le\|a\|_q\|x\|_p\).
For \(1\le p<\infty\), \((\ell^p)^*\cong\ell^q\).
The case \((\ell^\infty)^*\) is much larger than \(\ell^1\).
What can go wrong?

Boundaries of the theory

Infinite-dimensional spaces separate algebraic linearity from analytic continuity and make some familiar finite-dimensional identifications fail.

Omitted hypothesis

Linearity alone does not imply continuity in infinite dimensions.

\[D_1(p)=p'(1)\quad\text{on polynomials in }C[0,1]\text{ with }\|\cdot\|_\infty.\]
Failure.
\[p_n(x)=x^n,\qquad \|p_n\|_\infty=1,\qquad |D_1(p_n)|=n\to\infty.\]

No constant \(C\) can satisfy \(|p'(1)|\le C\|p\|_\infty\) for all polynomials.

Tempting statement

“Evaluation at a point should define a functional on every function space.”

\[\delta_t(f)=f(t).\]
Failure on \(L^p\).

Elements of \(L^p\) are equivalence classes modulo equality almost everywhere. Changing one point does not change the class, so \(f(t)\) is not well-defined. Even after choosing representatives, point evaluation is not controlled by the \(L^p\)-norm.

Two duals
\[X^\#=\{\text{all algebraic linear functionals}\},\qquad X^*=\{\text{continuous linear functionals}\}.\]
Infinite-dimensional distinction.

In finite dimensions, \(X^\#=X^*\). In infinite dimensions, discontinuous algebraic functionals exist under standard choice principles, so generally \(X^*\subsetneq X^\#\).

False inference

“Because \(\|\varphi\|_*\) is a supremum, some unit vector must attain it.”

Why the proof breaks.

The unit sphere need not be compact in infinite-dimensional normed spaces. A continuous functional always has a supremum over the unit ball, but norm attainment may fail. Compactness restores attainment in finite dimensions.

Common mistakes

Diagnostic corrections

Each correction separates the algebraic object from its topological or geometric representation.

Incorrect: \(X^*\) is the same set as \(X\).

There may be a natural identification in a Hilbert space through the Riesz representation theorem, but it depends on the inner product and is not the definition of the dual.

Correct: \(X^*\) consists of continuous scalar-valued linear maps on \(X\).
Incorrect: the annihilator \(W^\perp\) is automatically a subset of \(X\).

That notation resembles orthogonal complements, but the annihilator lives in \(X^*\).

Correct: \(W^\perp=\{\varphi\in X^*: \varphi|_W=0\}\).
Incorrect: every linear functional on any normed space is continuous.

This is true only in finite dimensions.

Correct: continuity is equivalent to a global bound \(|\varphi(x)|\le C\|x\|\).
Incorrect: the norm of \(\varphi_a(x)=a\cdot x\) is always \(\|a\|_2\).

The answer depends on the norm chosen on the primal space.

Correct: under \(\ell^p\), \(\|\varphi_a\|_*=\|a\|_q\), where \(1/p+1/q=1\).
Final synthesis
\[\boxed{\text{linear measurement}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{boundedness}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{dual norm}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{kernel and level sets}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{annihilator}}\]

The dual space packages every continuous linear observation available on \(X\). Its norm records maximal sensitivity, its kernels encode invisible directions, and annihilators describe which observations survive after a subspace is declared irrelevant.
