Relative interior and boundary

A segment in the plane has empty ordinary interior, yet it has a perfectly meaningful interior along its own line. Relative topology repairs the ambient mismatch and leads naturally to faces, supporting hyperplanes, and exposed boundary geometry.

1 · Mathematical question

What counts as “inside” a lower-dimensional set?

The relevant neighborhoods must live in the smallest affine space containing the set. Otherwise every segment, face, and singleton would appear to have no interior at all.

Ambient view

Too much space

A two-dimensional open ball around a point of a segment immediately leaves the segment.

Relative view

Correct intrinsic space

Intersect the ambient ball with the affine hull. Along that line, interior points of the segment have genuine neighborhoods.

Boundary geometry

Where support occurs

Faces are pieces of the boundary that convex combinations cannot enter without bringing their endpoints with them.

5 · Faces and relative interiors

Every point belongs to one smallest face

For a polytope, moving from the interior to an edge and then to a vertex lowers the dimension of the minimal face.

Interior point

The smallest face containing is itself.

Edge point

The smallest face is the entire edge, not merely the point.

Vertex

A vertex is a zero-dimensional face.

Face partition principle

For a polytope, the relative interiors of all faces form a disjoint partition: interior, open edges, and vertices.

8 · Extreme versus exposed

Every exposed point is extreme, but not conversely

A supporting functional may identify a whole edge, and some extreme points cannot be isolated by any support direction.

Non-exposed example

Consider the convex hull of the unit disk and the apex . The tangent-join points are extreme, but the unique supporting line at each also contains an entire boundary segment to the apex.

Diagnosis
Why non-exposed? At a tangent-join point, the only supporting line also contains the entire segment joining that point to the apex. Hence no linear functional has that point as its unique maximizer.

10 · What can go wrong?

Boundary statements that need qualification

Relative notions fix lower-dimensional pathologies, but faces and exposure still require careful distinctions.

Ordinary interior can be empty for structural reasons

A segment in has empty ordinary interior even though it is full-dimensional in its own affine hull.

Repair. Replace interior by relative interior and ambient balls by affine slices of balls.

An extreme point need not be exposed

The tangent-join points in the disk-plus-apex example are extreme but cannot be isolated by a supporting line.

Correct implication. Exposed points are extreme; the converse fails in general.

A boundary subset need not be a face

A curved arc of a disk boundary is not generally a face: a segment can have an interior point on the arc while its endpoints leave the arc.

Face test. If a relative interior point of a segment lies in the subset, both endpoints must lie there too.

Relative boundary depends on relative closure

For an open segment, the relative boundary consists of the two missing endpoints after closure is taken in the affine hull.

Do not use. alone is not the boundary when is not relatively closed.
11 · Common mistakes

Precision traps

Most errors come from forgetting the affine hull or confusing different types of boundary pieces.

Incorrect: “A set with empty interior has no interior points.” Correct: it may have a large relative interior inside its affine hull.
Incorrect: “Relative interior is computed using any smaller subspace containing the point.” Correct: it is computed specifically in .
Incorrect: “Every face is exposed.” Correct: every exposed face is a face, but non-exposed faces occur in general convex sets.
Incorrect: “Every boundary point is extreme.” Correct: points in the relative interior of an edge are boundary points but are not extreme.
